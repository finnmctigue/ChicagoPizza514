ChicagoPizza514: analysis of the current datasets

The usable primary combination is Google reviews plus Chicago food inspections. The local Yelp files contain no eligible Chicago restaurants. The sources support historical restaurant analysis after deduplication and business-link verification. They do not yet form a validated modeling dataset.

Audit date: November 22, 2026. Every raw file was scanned in full. Raw inputs and the existing notebook were left unchanged. File sizes below use decimal GB/MB; dates describe observations, not download or snapshot dates.

Google place scope and quality

The metadata has 179,205 rows and 178,204 unique places. All 1,001 extra rows duplicate the first full record for their ID; no conflicts were found. Address-based selection finds 37,127 Chicago places, of which 8,317 meet the documented restaurant rule. Another 365 cafe/cafeteria places form a separate possible expansion. The older pizza-only definition finds 705 places.

Core categories include Restaurant, categories ending in restaurant, Bar & grill, Diner, Sandwich shop, Bistro, and Crêperie. Category matching is case/accent/whitespace normalized. Restaurant supply stores do not qualify. A pizza delivery/takeaway-only category can be outside this restaurant definition, so the pizza cohort is not strictly nested within the core cohort. Geography uses Chicago/IL address text; no official boundary polygon was available.

Of core restaurants, 1,499 (18.0%) are marked permanently closed in the undated metadata. Preserve them for historical work. Metadata state is operating status, not geographic state. Median snapshot rating is 4.3; mean place-level snapshot rating is 4.17. These are undated summaries, not historical predictors.

These are overlapping candidate diagnostics, not accepted links. There are 1,078 Chicago license/address groups with multiple normalized DBA names and 319 licenses at multiple normalized addresses. 69 exact address/name candidates have their nearest matching inspection coordinate more than 100 meters away. Tenant units, shared buildings, chain branches, ownership changes, and historical aliases require review.

Only 1,092,427 deduplicated core reviews are on or after the first inspection of an exact-name/address candidate. This is a rough temporal diagnostic, not accepted linkage or proof of a prior inspection: it uses UTC dates and includes same-day records. 64,811 core inspections occur after the final statewide Google review date. They cannot be inspections preceding the reviews.

Keep source tables at their natural grains: one place, one review, one inspection. Build a reviewed source-place → restaurant episode → license crosswalk with evidence, confidence, decision, and supported dates. Do not join every review to every inspection, and do not join solely on address or license. For inspection prediction, use one row per eligible inspection with review features strictly before that inspection, a predefined lookback window, and Chicago local-date alignment. For retrospective comparisons, use restaurant-month aggregates with the latest accepted prior inspection.

Readiness, limitations, and next work

The repository currently contains raw sources, a preliminary profiler, a merge plan, and an exploratory notebook. No accepted business crosswalk or completed analytical table was present. The notebook reads only 1,000 metadata rows, while its Google review read has no row/chunk limit despite the preview comment. The saved code has no unfinished metadata.category.n expression and no saved errors; the merge plan’s notebook note is partly stale.

Recommended sequence: freeze restaurant/geography rules; deduplicate places and retained reviews; quarantine invalid dates and conflicting identities; build and manually validate business-episode links; export scoped unmatched tables as well as the accepted matched subset; then construct time-safe features. Evaluate chronological holdouts and restaurant-group leakage, compare against inspection-history baselines, and stratify performance by inspection type, time period, review volume, and matched/unmatched coverage.

No correlation, causal effect, predictive accuracy, sentiment score, or accepted restaurant match rate is asserted by this audit. Missing or conflicting reviews, unresolved historical identities, undated metadata, platform selection, popularity skew, code-definition changes, and review-era coverage limit those conclusions. Full scans establish the counts shown; they do not establish provenance, completeness of either platform, or that the cohort is a census of Chicago restaurants.

Reproduce: run python -u profile_current_datasets.py, then python build_dataset_report.py. The first script uses only the Python standard library and local normalization helpers; rendering requires matplotlib. Detailed aggregates and field schemas are in current_dataset_profile.json; supplemental inspection checks are in supplemental_inspection_profile.json.
