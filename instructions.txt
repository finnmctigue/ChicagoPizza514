MATCHING ILLINOIS GOOGLE METADATA TO CHICAGO FOOD INSPECTIONS

Source files
- meta-Illinois.json: newline-delimited JSON containing Google place metadata.
  Load it with pandas.read_json("meta-Illinois.json", lines=True).
- Food_Inspections_20261103.csv: individual food inspection records.

Recommended approach
Use normalized street addresses to find candidate businesses, then confirm
matches using business names and coordinates. There is no shared identifier:
Google uses gmap_id, while inspections use License #.

1. Prepare the metadata.
   Filter to Chicago and the categories relevant to your analysis. For a pizza
   project, start with categories containing "pizza". Inspect duplicate gmap_id
   records and resolve conflicting values before keeping one record per place.
   Keep the original names, addresses, and identifiers for auditing.

2. Normalize addresses in both datasets.
   Remove the business-name prefix from Google addresses and separate the street,
   city, state, and ZIP code. Standardize capitalization, punctuation, whitespace,
   street directions (North -> N), and street suffixes (Street -> ST).
   Keep suite and unit numbers separately so they can help resolve ambiguity.
   Some Google addresses include a building name before the street address;
   taking the first comma-separated field will miss these cases. Handle street
   number ranges and letter suffixes explicitly rather than discarding them.

3. Match candidates at the same normalized street address.
   Compare Google's name against both DBA Name and AKA Name. Normalize case,
   punctuation, accents, and whitespace in names. Accept clear, unique matches
   after checking for conflicting businesses; flag ambiguous matches for review.
   Use city and ZIP as supporting evidence, allowing for missing or incorrect
   values. Retain historical name variants and their inspection dates.

4. Recover remaining matches.
   Use fuzzy name comparisons among candidates at the same address. For address
   discrepancies, look for nearby coordinates, initially within about 100 meters,
   and require supporting name or address evidence. This distance is a starting
   point to calibrate by reviewing examples, not a validated acceptance rule.
   Do not match on proximity or a chain name alone. Review candidates with
   multiple plausible matches, and leave unsupported records unmatched.

5. Save a mapping table before joining inspection records.
   Include gmap_id, License #, inspection-side name and address, matching method,
   name similarity, geographic distance, and review status. Include inspection
   dates or the relevant business period when needed to distinguish historical
   operators. Match business identities before attaching their inspection history;
   do not fuzzy-match each inspection independently. Preserve Inspection ID so
   the final joined table can be checked for accidental duplication.

6. Attach only the inspections belonging to the matched business.
   A place may have multiple inspections and may have multiple historical
   licenses. An address or license alone does not establish business identity.
   Check names, addresses, and dates before attaching records. For a restaurant-
   level analysis, aggregate inspections within an explicitly defined date window
   before combining them with one metadata record per Google place.

Initial profile of the local files
These counts came from a preliminary normalization pass on November 3, 2026.
They describe candidate matches, not verified business links, and will change
with improved address parsing or updated source files.

- Metadata records statewide: 179,205.
- Unique gmap_id values statewide: 178,204.
- Unique Chicago Google places: 37,127.
- Inspection records: 317,961.
- Unique Chicago Google places with a category containing "pizza": 705.
- Of those 705 places, 577 have an exact normalized street-address candidate.
- Of those 705 places, 274 also have an exact normalized name match against
  an inspection DBA Name or AKA Name at that address.
- Of those 705 places, 416 have multiple inspection licenses at their matching
  street address. This includes records that also have an exact name match.

Important cautions
- Never assign every inspection at an address to one Google place. For example,
  945 W Randolph St has inspection records for Tomato Head, Blackwood BBQ,
  Umami Burger, and Voodoo Doughnut.
- Some licenses are associated with different business names over time, so a
  license-only join can attach another operator's inspection history.
- Shared buildings, food courts, chains, rebranding, ownership changes, and
  closed businesses require extra review.
- The metadata has no explicit snapshot date. Its ratings cannot be assumed to
  describe the business in 2026. Define and justify the inspection date window
  when analyzing ratings versus inspection results.
